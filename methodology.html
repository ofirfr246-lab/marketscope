Data Sources & Methodology

Where our data comes from, how scores are calculated, and what a verdict does and doesn't mean.

Last updated: November 12, 2026 · Early-access version

Our principle

We don't sell a promise that a product will succeed. We sell better information, structured analysis, and smarter decisions. Every report is designed to be explainable.

Data sources

Depending on your plan, analyses draw on:

  • Google Shopping: listings, prices, stores
  • Google Search: search demand, trends, seasonality
  • Meta Ad Library: advertising presence (Plus, Pro)
  • TikTok: content and trend signals (Plus, Pro)
  • Amazon: marketplace competition and pricing (Pro)
  • AliExpress: supply-side pricing (Pro)

Data is obtained from publicly available sources, official APIs and licensed data providers. Coverage varies by market and product.

The four scores

  • Demand (0–100): search volume, growth and seasonality for the product in the market.
  • Competition (0–100): number of sellers, market concentration and pricing pressure. Higher means harder.
  • Saturation (0–100): listing density relative to demand, across price bands. Higher means more crowded.
  • Opportunity (0–100): a weighted combination of the above, rewarding strong demand with room to compete.

Verdicts

The verdict maps the Opportunity score and confidence into five levels: STRONG BUY, BUY, HOLD, UNDERPERFORM and AVOID. Confidence reflects how much data was available and how consistent the signals were.

AI Advisor

The AI Advisor summarizes the data in plain language and highlights risks, opportunities and gaps. It is instructed to ground statements in the report's data, but AI can make mistakes. Review important conclusions before acting.

Limitations

  • Data reflects the moment of collection and markets change quickly.
  • Some sources provide estimates, samples or partial coverage.
  • Scores don't account for your brand, execution, margins, supply chain or marketing.
  • Past trends don't guarantee future results.

Store Intelligence

Store Intelligence is an early-access way to look up a competitor’s public storefront. In a future live mode it will use only publicly available information: public product listings and public ad libraries (for example the Meta Ad Library). We do not collect personal data about a store’s customers, and we do not bypass logins, paywalls, anti-bot protections or other access controls. We will not access private dashboards or authenticated areas of a site.

The profiles on this site today are labelled example or placeholder data. Live collection is not connected yet. Platform detection from a pasted URL is client-side pattern matching only — we do not fetch the store when you paste the link in this preview.
