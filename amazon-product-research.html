Amazon product research vs Google Shopping demand

Two different demand surfaces, what each can show, and what MarketScope can measure today. Amazon listings, BSR and review velocity are coming soon — we will not invent those figures.

Amazon product research and Google demand are not the same job

Amazon product research usually means studying a marketplace: listings on Amazon, how crowded a niche looks there, prices, reviews, and — when you have the data — signals such as Best Seller Rank and review velocity. Google Shopping and Google Search demand answer a different question: are people looking for this product on Google, and what shopping listings do they already see?

Those two surfaces overlap, but they are not substitutes. A product can be loud on Amazon and quiet in Google Search, or the other way around. People also research on Google and then buy elsewhere — on Amazon, on a brand site, or on a store they found in Shopping. If you only watch one surface, you can mistake a channel-specific pattern for “the market.”

MarketScope is built to help you test a product in a named market with structured scores. Today, the live sources behind those scores are Google Shopping and Google Search. Amazon product research inside the product is planned, and it is not live yet. This guide is about that distinction, not about inventing Amazon numbers we do not have.

What each source can show — and what it cannot

Google Search is useful for demand language, volume, trend and a sense of traffic cost (for example, CPC). Google Shopping is useful for the public offer landscape off Amazon: listing count, seller count, prices, and how concentrated the visible stores are. Together they help answer “is anyone looking, and what do they find on Google?”

Amazon, when you can measure it properly, is useful for marketplace competition: how many similar listings are on Amazon, how they are priced, how reviews have accumulated, and how a category is moving. That is why so many sellers start product research on Amazon. It is a real buying venue, with its own fees, rules and search ranking.

Neither source, on its own, proves that you will make money. They do not know your landed cost, your shipping time, your creative, or how well you will run ads or a listing. They also do not transfer cleanly across countries. US Amazon and US Google are already two views; another country is another study. For how MarketScope turns the live Google signals into scores, see how to read scores and verdicts.

What MarketScope measures today

Live reports use Google Shopping and Google Search only. That is enough to score demand, competition, saturation and opportunity on the Google surface, and to produce a verdict and an advisor summary. On Starter and above, reports also include a price-band recommendation from the Shopping data.

That is a genuine product research pass, especially for dropshippers and DTC founders who will sell on their own store, and for anyone who wants to know whether search demand exists before they spend on ads. It is not yet a full Amazon product research desk.

  • Amazon BSR and review velocity — planned on Starter and up.

We will not preview them with invented BSR figures, review counts or supplier costs. If a report does not include Amazon, it should say so, and the public demo does.

Why Google demand still matters for Amazon sellers

If you plan to sell on Amazon, you might ask why a Google Shopping and Search report is worth running at all. A few honest reasons:

  • Many shoppers start on Google, even when they finish on Amazon. Search demand can confirm that people look for the product by name, not only that Amazon already has listings for it.
  • A crowded Amazon niche and a quiet Google query (or the reverse) is itself information. It may change how you title a listing, whether you also run a store, or whether you walk away.
  • If you might sell on your own site as well as Amazon, Google Shopping is the comparable public shelf: prices, sellers and listing density off the Amazon SERP.
  • You can do this check today, with live data, while Amazon inside MarketScope is still being built.

None of that replaces Amazon-specific work. Fees, FBA or FBM, review density and keyword ranking on Amazon remain Amazon work. The point is not to treat Google as a stand-in. The point is to treat it as a second, currently available surface.

A real report — and what it does not contain

The public demo is a magnetic phone mount in the US, generated on 12 November 2026 from live Google Shopping and Google Search only. In that run, Search showed about 8,100 monthly searches and a roughly stable 12-month trend, with a CPC of about $1.21. Demand scored 76/100. Shopping showed 65 listings from 29 sellers, a price range of $6.68–$69, a median of $19.99, and a recommended band of $14.99–$19.99. Competition scored 90/100, saturation 64/100, opportunity 48/100. The verdict was UNDERPERFORM.

The same page is explicit about what is missing: ad-library and TikTok modules are not in the report, and “Amazon listings, BSR and review velocity are also coming soon.” We are not going to add an estimated Amazon rank or a review count here. Those figures were not collected. If you need Amazon evidence for that product, you have to look at Amazon yourself until our collector is live.

Even on Google data alone, the report is already a caution. Solid search demand did not cancel out high competition. That is useful for an Amazon founder too: a product people search for can still be a hard shelf to enter.

Until Amazon data is live

If Amazon is your main channel, use MarketScope for the Google-side demand and pricing check, then do a manual Amazon pass before you source inventory. Keep that pass boring and written down:

  • Search the exact product name in your target Amazon marketplace and note how many close offers you can see, and how they are priced.
  • Read recent reviews for quality complaints and for signs of durable demand — without treating review totals as a sales figure.
  • Run the fee and landed-cost math yourself. MarketScope’s margin tools are planned on Pro with AliExpress, and they are not live.
  • Do not copy a “winning products” list into Amazon and call it research. The winning-products guide and the dropshipping checklist both start with a named product and a named market, not a scraped trend list.

When Amazon ships in MarketScope, it will be labelled live, and reports that include it should say so. Until then, “Coming soon” means coming soon.
