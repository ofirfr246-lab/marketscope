AliExpress vs Alibaba vs 1688: which sourcing platform to use

A structural comparison of AliExpress, Alibaba.com and 1688 for ecommerce sourcing: who each marketplace is for, when to move, and how to compare landed cost — not invented markups.

Three platforms, three jobs

AliExpress, Alibaba.com and 1688 are often described as interchangeable “China suppliers.” They are not. They are different marketplaces, aimed at different buyers, with different languages, payment methods and minimum orders. Choosing the wrong one wastes time: you will try to dropship from a wholesale RFQ, or you will compare a retail export price with a domestic carton price and think you have found a secret margin.

This comparison stays at the structural level — who each site is for, and when to move from one to another. It does not invent typical markups, shipping times or MOQs for every category. Always read the listing, the contract and the Incoterm in front of you. For the cost math, use landed cost and margin.

Those price comparisons are directional, not a multiplier. A cheap 1688 listing can become expensive after agent fees, export packing, international freight, duties and quality failures. A higher AliExpress price can be the right one if you need one unit this week.

AliExpress: testing and small international orders

AliExpress is Alibaba Group’s international retail marketplace. You can search in a familiar language, pay with common international methods, read reviews, and order one piece with shipping to many countries. That is why it is the usual first stop for dropshippers and for founders who want to test a product without a carton of inventory.

It is the right starting point when you need:

  • A single sample or a handful of test orders.
  • International checkout without negotiating a manufacturing run.
  • A first look at reviews, photos and variant options.
  • A supplier who will ship to your customer, if you are dropshipping.

The trade-offs are structural. Unit prices are usually higher than on domestic wholesale platforms. Many listings use generic packaging. Processing and transit times vary by seller, and the person behind a storefront may be a reseller, not a factory. The shipping date on the listing is a claim until you measure it with an order to yourself.

AliExpress is a poor place to stop if you already have consistent demand and you are still paying retail-export prices on every order. That is the moment to compare a wholesale quote.

Alibaba.com: wholesale, samples and customization

Alibaba.com is a business-to-business marketplace. Sellers generally expect a company or a serious reseller, and many listings show a minimum order quantity. You typically talk to the supplier about specifications, materials, packaging, lead times and payment terms. That conversation is the point of the platform.

Use Alibaba.com when you need:

  • A wholesale price that moves with quantity.
  • A formal sample (including paid samples) before a bulk order.
  • OEM or ODM work: a logo, a colorway, a carton, a manual, a small design change.
  • A written agreement on Incoterms, lead time and quality checks.

It is usually the wrong first click if you only want one unit tomorrow. Some sellers will sell a sample, many will not, and the process is slower than retail checkout. “Gold Supplier” or similar badges are not a substitute for a sample, a video inspection, or a small trial order. Treat every factory claim as unverified until the goods in your hands match the specification.

Payment terms and trade-assurance style protections vary by order and are defined by the platform’s current policies and by your contract. Read those terms; do not rely on a summary in a blog post (including this one) as the rules of your deal.

1688: domestic Chinese wholesale, usually with help

1688.com is Alibaba Group’s domestic wholesale platform in China. Listings are aimed at buyers inside that market: the language is Chinese, prices are typically in yuan, and shipping is set up for domestic delivery. That is why unit prices are often lower than the export-facing listings for a similar item.

International sellers usually reach 1688 through:

  • A sourcing agent or purchasing office that buys, inspects, consolidates and exports.
  • A fulfillment partner that already buys on 1688 and ships to customers or to a warehouse.
  • An in-house Chinese-speaking buyer with a workable payment and export path.

Agents charge for their work — a fee, a service margin, or both — and they introduce a second relationship you have to manage. Quality control, photo inspection, and a clear specification matter more here, not less, because you may never see the goods before they leave China. A lower unit price that arrives with the wrong voltage, the wrong fabric, or no branding is not a saving.

1688 is a poor first platform if you cannot read the listing, cannot pay, and have no one who will receive the goods in China. In that situation, AliExpress or a sample from Alibaba.com is the more honest start.

When to move from one platform to the next

  1. Discover and test on AliExpress (or from a local/retail source) with a sample to yourself and, if you dropship, a small number of customer orders.
  2. Validate demand and contribution margin using the steps in how to validate a product idea. If the product fails here, do not “fix it” by moving to 1688.
  3. Quote wholesale on Alibaba.com for the same specification. Compare landed cost at the quantities you can actually fund, not at a fantasy container size.
  4. Check 1688 when the product is proven and you have a reliable way to buy, inspect and export. Use the same specification sheet so you are comparing the same item.

It is normal to use more than one platform at the same time: AliExpress for a variant you still want to test, Alibaba.com for the core SKU, 1688 for packaging or a mature item. What is not normal is switching platforms only to chase a lower screenshot price.

Quality, IP and the same product problem

The same photos appear on all three sites, often because resellers share supplier images. You are not necessarily looking at three factories. Before you move volume:

  • Order a sample of the exact SKU and color you will sell.
  • Write down materials, dimensions, accessories, labels and pack-out, and require the next batch to match.
  • Check whether the design, logo or character is yours to sell in the destination market.
  • Confirm electrical, battery, cosmetic or children’s product requirements for that market.

If two “factories” send goods that look like they came from the same line, you may have less backup than you think. A true second source should be able to match the specification independently.

Frequently asked questions

Is 1688 always cheaper than AliExpress or Alibaba?

Domestic wholesale unit prices on 1688 are often lower, but your landed cost includes agent fees, export, freight, duties, defects and time. Compare all-in cost for the same specification, at the quantity you will actually buy.

Can I dropship directly from 1688?

Usually not in the same way as from AliExpress. 1688 is built for domestic buyers. Most international sellers use an agent or a fulfillment partner to purchase, inspect and forward goods.

Should I start on Alibaba.com if I want private label?

Alibaba.com is the usual place to discuss customization and wholesale once you know the product. Validate demand and sell a testable version first, unless you have a clear reason to fund tooling and inventory before any signal.

How do I know if a supplier is a factory?

You do not know from a badge or a chat message. Samples, consistent specifications, inspection photos or visits, and whether they can explain production lead times are more useful than labels. Many sellers on all three platforms are traders.

Which platform should I use for a first product test?

For most first tests, an international retail channel such as AliExpress (or a local supplier) is the simplest way to get one unit and measure delivery. Move to Alibaba.com or 1688 after the idea survives demand and margin checks.
